# Scene Lighting Rules

Use this reference to match lighting choices to scene type.

## Interview / Founder / Consultant

Goal: trustworthy face, dimensional background, controlled authority.

- Use a large soft key 30-45 degrees from camera, slightly above eye line.
- Keep fill low enough to preserve facial structure.
- Add subtle rim or hair light if background is dark.
- Use warm practicals or background gradient to avoid a dead wall.
- Avoid flat frontal webcam light unless the user wants livestream realism.

## Education / Course / Consulting Video

Goal: clear, friendly, professional, not over-dramatic.

- Use soft frontal-side key rather than dramatic split light.
- Keep shadows shallow but visible.
- Add background separation through practical lamp, bookcase glow, or wall gradient.
- Skin tone must stay natural.
- Avoid heavy noir, neon, or extreme color casts.

## Product Ad

Goal: make material and selling point readable.

- Glass/liquid: backlight and edge highlights, controlled reflections, dark cards for shape.
- Metal: hard or semi-hard specular highlights, precise flags, black/white reflection control.
- Fabric/leather: soft grazing side light to reveal texture.
- Food: soft side/back light, warm highlights, fresh shadow detail.
- Tech product: cool clean key, thin rim light, controlled background gradient.

## Beauty / Fashion

Goal: flattering face, clean skin, sculpted bone structure.

- Use butterfly light, clamshell light, or large soft front key.
- Add catchlights in eyes.
- Keep shadows clean and gentle unless editorial drama is requested.
- Avoid hard top light that creates tired eye sockets.

## Suspense / Thriller / Noir

Goal: uncertainty, concealment, pressure.

- Use side key, split lighting, hard practicals, venetian-blind shadows, top light, or low-key contrast.
- Leave controlled darkness; do not fill everything.
- Use motivated sources: corridor light, desk lamp, streetlight, car headlight, screen.
- Background should contain depth pockets, not empty black mud.

## Night Interior

Goal: believable darkness with readable subject.

- Pick one dominant motivation: lamp, moon window, screen, neon, candle.
- Reinforce the source off-camera without making it obvious.
- Use warm/cool contrast when interior practical meets exterior moon/city light.
- Add negative fill to keep night from becoming gray.

## Night Exterior / City

Goal: motivated urban light and separation.

- Use streetlight pools, store signs, neon reflections, car sweeps, wet pavement, moon/sky ambient.
- Keep faces readable through motivated edge or bounce.
- Let background lights create depth.
- Avoid evenly lighting the whole street unless it is a commercial fantasy look.

## Action / Fight / Chase

Goal: readable bodies, motion direction, impact, and danger.

- Use strong back/rim light to separate moving bodies from background.
- Use practical pools or directional shafts to define spatial zones.
- Avoid over-dark lighting that hides choreography.
- For dust, smoke, rain, or particles, use backlight to reveal volume.

## VFX / CG / Fantasy

Goal: integrate effect with scene physically.

- Make the effect emit or respond to light if it should.
- Describe contact light, bounce, reflections, shadows, volumetric glow, and exposure falloff.
- Keep light interaction consistent across skin, clothing, props, floor, smoke, water, and particles.

## Documentary / Realism

Goal: invisible craft, believable space.

- Use existing window/room sources and subtle bounce/negative fill.
- Keep color temperature realistic.
- Avoid overly polished rim lights unless the frame already supports them.
- Let imperfection remain if it increases truthfulness.
